Abstract

Surgical scene reconstruction is crucial for applications such as intraoperative navigation and robotic surgery. Most existing methods rely on self-supervised depth estimation via the photometric consistency assumption. However, they perform poorly on smooth, textureless biological tissues due to violations of this assumption caused by illumination changes and non-Lambertian reflectance.

To address this problem, we propose SurgMASt3R-SLAM, a novel pipeline that adapts a 3D foundation model, MASt3R, for surgical scene reconstruction. To solve the problem of illumination changes, MASt3R firstly apply low-rank adaption to efficiently finetune MASt3R on data with ground-truths while reserves the generalization ability for zero-shot reconstruction on unseen data.

To improve fine-tuning efficiency, we propose to select frame pairs in sliding windows to ensure sufficient visual overlap, addressing the challenge of limited field-of-view and frequent occlusions in surgical scenes. Moreover, the finetuned MASt3R combined with a SLAM system is capable of handling extended surgical video sequences to reconstruct the whole scene that runs at 10 FPS. Experimental results on SimCol and generalization testing on C3VD and SCARED datasets show that SurgMASt3R-SLAM achieves competitive performance in depth estimation, pose estimation, and 3D reconstruction.


Method

In contrast to conventional SSL depth-centric approaches above, our work targets holistic 3D scene reconstruction by leveraging a pre-trained 3D foundation model with a SLAM system in a supervised paradigm. Rather than treating depth estimation in isolation, we treat depth as one attribute within a comprehensive set of scene properties, generating depth after predicting camera parameters and global 3D structure. The whole pipeline is divided into 3 phases:

Phase 1: Finetune MASt3R in a supervised paradigm. To effectively adapt this representation to the surgical domain with minimal data, we first employ parameter-efficient fine-tuning via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to MASt3R using a small amount of surgical data with ground-truths, substantially reducing computational cost and mitigating overfitting. This allows the model to capture characteristic geometric properties of surgical scenes, such as smooth and low-textured tissue surfaces, while preserving the foundation model's strong generalization capability for unseen surgical data. Furthermore, to enhance geometric consistency and data efficiency during fine-tuning, we introduce a strategic image pair selection method that increases image pairs' overlap and reinforces robust learning on challenging tissue surfaces.

Phase 2: Combine Finetuned MASt3R with a SLAM system. By integrating the adapted representation into a deep learning-based Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) framework, our approach efficiently handles extended surgical video sequences and achieves real-time 3D scene reconstruction. Unlike Structure from Motion (SfM) methods, which also enable global optimization but suffer from low reconstruction speed and are impractical for real-time processing, our SLAM-based solution offers a scalable approach for surgical stationary scene reconstruction, depth, and pose estimation.

Phase 3: Generating depth under the prediction of pose and 3D point cloud. To compare our work with present depth estimation methods quantitatively, we induced depth after estimating the camera parameters and 3D point cloud. By leveraging MASt3R, SurgMASt3R-SLAM is capable of handling both cases with known camera parameters and cases where camera parameters are unknown.

Experimental Results

We evaluate SurgMASt3R-SLAM on three datasets: SimCol (28,776 training frames, 9,009 test frames), C3VD (22 colonic videos with 8 selected for testing), and SCARED dataset. With intrinsics, SurgMASt3R-SLAM achieves superior depth estimation performance on both SimCol (train and test) and C3VD (zero-shot test) datasets, outperforming other methods across most metrics.

Our method achieves Abs Rel of 0.046 on SimCol and 0.083 on C3VD, demonstrating robust performance in both controlled and unseen settings. The system operates at approximately 10 FPS, delivering high-fidelity 3D reconstructions in real time for practical deployment in surgical environments.
